Introduction

Bharathapuzha, popularly known as Nila, is one of the most important rivers in Kerala. It is the second-longest river in the state after the Periyar River. The river originates in the Anaimalai Hills of the Western Ghats in Tamil Nadu and flows westward through Kerala before joining the Arabian Sea near Ponnani in Malappuram district.


Bharathapuzha has a significant role in the geographical, agricultural, ecological and cultural development of Kerala. It supports agricultural activities, provides water resources and sustains several settlements along its course. The river is closely associated with the history and cultural traditions of the Malabar region.


The river is also known as Nila and is considered an important part of Kerala's cultural identity. Its banks are associated with traditional art forms, literature, festivals and historical settlements.

Physical and Geographical Features

1. Physiographic Setting

The Bharathapuzha River originates in the Anaimalai Hills of the Western Ghats. Its basin extends from the elevated mountain region in the east towards the coastal plains in the west. The river passes through different physiographic divisions, including highlands, midlands and lowlands.

2. Drainage Basin

The total drainage basin covers approximately 6,186 square kilometres. About 4,400 square kilometres lie within Kerala, while the remaining area extends into Tamil Nadu. It is one of the major river basins of Kerala.

3. Major Tributaries

The important tributaries of Bharathapuzha include Gayathripuzha, Kannadipuzha, Kalpathipuzha and Thuthapuzha. These tributaries contribute water to the main river and form an extensive drainage network.

4. Climate and Rainfall

The river basin experiences seasonal rainfall, particularly during the southwest monsoon. Rainfall in the Western Ghats contributes to the flow of the river. The middle and lower portions experience comparatively drier conditions in certain seasons.

5. Land Use and Agriculture

The Bharathapuzha basin supports various agricultural activities. Paddy cultivation, coconut plantations and other crops are common in different parts of the basin. River water is also important for irrigation and domestic requirements.

Importance of Bharathapuzha

Bharathapuzha plays an important role in the environmental, economic and cultural life of Kerala. It provides water for irrigation, agriculture and domestic purposes. The river basin supports diverse ecosystems and provides habitats for different species of plants and animals.


The river also has considerable cultural significance. It is closely connected with the traditional identity of the Malabar region. Kerala Kalamandalam, located near the river at Cheruthuruthy, is an important institution associated with Kerala's classical performing arts.


Conservation of Bharathapuzha is essential for maintaining ecological balance, protecting biodiversity and ensuring the sustainable use of water resources. Measures such as preventing pollution, protecting riverbanks and promoting responsible water management are important for its long-term conservation.
